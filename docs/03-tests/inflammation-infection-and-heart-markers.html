Medical Literacy Handbook (India)

Inflammation, Infection and Heart Marker Tests

Status: Draft. A doctor has not reviewed it yet. Last checked: November 2026.

Read How lab reports work and The immune system first.

Note

Quick summary

  • CRP and ESR show that something is inflamed. They cannot say what. A normal value does not exclude disease.
  • Infection tests work in different ways: grow the germ (culture), detect its parts (antigen, PCR), or detect your antibodies (serology). Timing and sample matter.
  • Some popular Indian tests are poor guides. The Widal test for typhoid is unreliable; blood culture is the better test.
  • Troponin is the key blood marker of heart-muscle injury; BNP helps with heart failure; D-dimer helps rule out clots.

Part A: Inflammation markers

1. CRP (C-reactive protein)
What it measuresA protein the liver makes within hours of inflammation or infection
Why the body makes itPart of the early defence; helps immune cells clear germs and damaged cells
Can be raised byBacterial infection (often high), viral infection (often modest), injury, surgery, heart attack, autoimmune disease, cancers, obesity (mildly)
Can be lowered byTreating the cause; steroids and anti-inflammatory drugs
What else you needSymptoms, WBC, the trend over days
Example rangeOften below about 5 to 10 mg/L; very high values (above 100) suggest serious infection or inflammation ⚠ verify

hs-CRP (high-sensitivity CRP) is a more sensitive version, used to look at low-grade inflammation and heart risk (values below about 1 mg/L are low-risk; above 3 higher). It is a different test with different units from ordinary CRP; do not mix them up. ⚠ verify

2. ESR (erythrocyte sedimentation rate)
What it measuresHow fast red cells settle in a tall tube in one hour (mm/hr); faster when proteins from inflammation are high
Can be raised byInfection, inflammation, anaemia, pregnancy, older age, kidney disease, some cancers
Can be lowered byVery high red cell count, some blood conditions
What else you needCRP, symptoms
NotesRises slowly and falls slowly, so it is less useful in acute illness than CRP. The upper limit rises with age (a rough rule: age ÷ 2 for men, (age + 10) ÷ 2 for women). ⚠ verify

3. Procalcitonin

A marker that rises more in serious bacterial infection than in viral illness. In hospitals it helps decide whether antibiotics are needed or can be stopped. It is not a routine outpatient test.

4. Misled about inflammation tests

What happensWhat to do
"ESR 25, you have a hidden problem"Mildly raised ESR is common (age, anaemia, women). Ask what condition is suspected and whether other tests are needed
Normal CRP read as "no problem"Some conditions are quiet on CRP; symptoms still matter
Antibiotics "because CRP is up"CRP rises in many non-bacterial problems; ask what infection is suspected
hs-CRP for everyoneUsed only as an add-on for selected heart-risk decisions

Part B: Infection tests

5. Four ways a test looks for infection

MethodHow it worksGood forLimits
CultureGrows the germ from blood, urine, stool, sputum or pusIdentifying the exact germ and which antibiotics work (sensitivity)Takes 1 to several days; must be taken before antibiotics; contamination possible
Antigen test (including many rapid tests)Detects a piece of the germQuick answers (dengue NS1, malaria, strep)Can miss early or low-level infection; false positives possible
PCR / NAAT (nucleic acid test)Detects the germ's genetic materialVery sensitive and specific for many viruses and for TB (Xpert/CBNAAT)Costly; may stay positive after the germ is dead
Serology (antibody tests)Detects your antibodies (IgM, IgG)Past infection, immunity, some current infectionsAntibodies take days to appear, so early tests may be negative; IgG can stay positive for years

Reading antibody results

  • IgM usually appears first and suggests a recent infection. IgG appears later and can mean past infection or vaccination.
  • A positive IgG does not mean the infection is active.
  • The window period is the time after infection before a test can detect it; a negative result too early can be a false reassurance, so repeat if suspicion remains.

Culture reports

A culture lists the organism and a table of antibiotics marked S (sensitive: likely to work), I (intermediate) or R (resistant: unlikely to work). Ask your doctor to use the report to choose a narrow antibiotic. A mixed or "contaminated" growth is not a true infection.

6. Common infections and their tests (India)

Typhoid (enteric fever)

TestNotes
Blood cultureThe best test; ideally before antibiotics. Sensitivity is not perfect, so a negative does not always exclude it. ⚠ verify
Widal testUnreliable. In India many healthy people have raised antibody levels from past exposure or vaccination, and cross-reactions with other fevers are common. It is widely used but discouraged by expert bodies. ⚠ verify
Newer rapid antibody testsLimited accuracy; not a substitute for culture

Dengue

TestWhen it helps
NS1 antigenUsually positive in the first several days of fever
IgM antibodyBecomes positive around day 4 to 5 onward
CBCPlatelet trend and haematocrit are key to following severity (see CBC)

Most dengue is mild; the dangerous period is often when the fever falls (days 3 to 7), with warning signs such as belly pain, repeated vomiting, bleeding, drowsiness, a rising haematocrit with falling platelets, or fluid in the belly or chest. ⚠ verify

Malaria

  • Blood smear (microscope) and rapid antigen tests (RDT) are standard. Repeat testing may be needed if suspicion is high and the first is negative. Fever with chills in an endemic area should be tested rather than guessed.

Tuberculosis (TB)

TestWhat it shows
Sputum smear and Xpert/CBNAATDetects TB germs or their genes and rifampicin resistance
CultureConfirms and tests drug resistance
Chest X-raySuggestive changes
Mantoux (tuberculin skin test) and IGRAShow exposure or latent infection, not active disease. Prior BCG vaccination can cause a false-positive Mantoux; IGRA is not affected by BCG.

A positive Mantoux or IGRA alone does not mean you have TB disease.

Viral hepatitis and HIV

  • Hepatitis B (HBsAg and antibodies) and hepatitis C (anti-HCV, then PCR to confirm active infection) are blood tests; see Liver and fatty liver.
  • HIV testing is done with counselling and a confirmatory test.

Viral fevers

Most fevers with a cough, runny nose or sore throat are viral. Blood tests are often normal or non-specific. Antibiotics do not help viral illness.

7. Misled about infection tests

What happensWhat to do
Treating typhoid on a single Widal titreAsk for a blood culture, ideally before antibiotics
Antibiotics started for "viral fever" with a normal or mildly raised CRPAsk what bacterial infection is suspected and what the antibiotic is for
A positive IgG read as "active infection"IgG often means past exposure
A positive Mantoux read as "TB"Latent infection or BCG; active TB needs sputum and imaging
Many "fever panel" combinations for every feverAsk which tests are justified by your symptoms and days of illness
Culture sent after several antibiotic dosesThe result may be falsely negative; give the sample first when possible

Part C: Heart and clot markers

8. Troponin: card
What it measuresA protein inside heart muscle cells (troponin I or T) that leaks into blood when those cells are injured
Why the body has itIt helps heart muscle contract; it is not meant to be in blood
Can be raised byHeart attack; also other heart muscle injury (myocarditis, heart failure, severe infection), a pulmonary embolism, kidney failure, and very hard endurance exercise
Can be lowered byIt falls over days after an injury ends
What else you needSymptoms, the ECG, and a repeat troponin a few hours later (a rising pattern is key)
NotesBy the Fourth Universal Definition of MI (2018), a heart attack needs a rise and/or fall of troponin with at least one value above the 99th percentile, plus evidence of heart-muscle oxygen shortage (symptoms, ECG or imaging). High-sensitivity troponin tests detect very small rises; a raised value is not always a heart attack, so it is interpreted with the rest of the picture.

9. CK-MB, BNP and NT-proBNP

TestWhat it shows
CK-MBAn older marker of heart muscle damage; mostly replaced by troponin
BNP / NT-proBNPReleased when the heart's chambers are stretched. High in heart failure. Also raised by kidney disease, age and some lung conditions. A normal value makes heart failure unlikely.

10. D-dimer: card
What it measuresA fragment released when a blood clot dissolves
Can be raised byClots in the leg veins or lungs; also pregnancy, infection, cancer, recent surgery, older age, inflammation
What else you needA clinical probability assessment
UseA normal D-dimer helps rule OUT a clot in low-risk people. A high D-dimer does not prove a clot; it needs imaging.

11. Misled about heart tests

What happensWhat to do
A normal ECG taken as "heart is fine"ECG can be normal between episodes; ask about stress tests or other tests if symptoms suggest it
A "heart package" for a young healthy personAsk which tests change management
One mildly raised troponin treated as a heart attackAsk for a repeat and the clinical correlation
D-dimer ordered for vague breathlessness without assessmentAsk what the clot probability is

12. Ask your doctor

  1. What condition are you looking for with this test?
  2. Does my timing of illness suit this test (early dengue NS1, antibodies later)?
  3. Do we need a culture before antibiotics?
  4. Does a positive antibody mean active infection or past exposure?
  5. If a heart marker is raised, will we repeat it, and what other tests complete the picture?
  6. What will we do differently if the result is normal or abnormal?

13. Red flags

Emergency: chest pressure, breathlessness, fainting, sudden calf swelling with breathlessness, confusion with high fever, a stiff neck with fever and headache, a rash that does not fade when pressed, fever with severe belly pain and bleeding, a baby with fever who is drowsy or not feeding.

Key terms

CRP / hs-CRP inflammation protein / its sensitive version · ESR how fast red cells settle · Procalcitonin a bacterial-infection marker · Culture growing the germ · Sensitivity (S/I/R) which antibiotics work · Antigen a germ piece · PCR / NAAT a genetic-material test · Serology antibody testing · IgM / IgG early / lasting antibodies · Window period the early period when a test may miss infection · Widal an unreliable typhoid antibody test · NS1 a dengue antigen · Mantoux / IGRA TB exposure tests · Troponin a heart-muscle-injury marker · BNP / NT-proBNP heart-stretch markers · D-dimer a clot-breakdown fragment.
