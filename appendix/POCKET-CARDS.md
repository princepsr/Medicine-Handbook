# Pocket Cards

*One-page summaries to print or save on your phone. They are **reminders, not diagnosis.** Reference ranges are **examples** that vary by lab, age, sex and pregnancy; always read the range printed on your own report. Every card links to the chapter with the full explanation and its sources.*

---

## Card 1: Emergency numbers and when to go to hospital now

| Number | Use |
|---|---|
| **112** | National emergency number |
| **108** | Ambulance (many states) |
| **Tele-MANAS 14416** | Free mental health helpline `⚠ verify` |
| **1800 116 117** | National Poisons Information Centre (AIIMS) `⚠ verify` |

**Go to the emergency department or call 112 or 108 for:** chest pain or pressure; trouble breathing; **BE-FAST stroke signs** (Balance, Eyes, Face, Arm, Speech, Time); sudden severe headache; heavy bleeding; vomiting blood or black stools; fainting; a seizure; severe injury; a snake or animal bite; swelling of the face or throat after a medicine, sting or food; a very sick or drowsy child; thoughts of self-harm.
Details: [First aid and emergencies](../10-practical/first-aid-and-emergencies.md), [Stroke](../02-diseases/stroke.md).

## Card 2: Ten questions for any doctor visit

1. What is the diagnosis, in simple words?
2. Which test result supports it? My value and the normal range?
3. Is this an emergency? What if I wait?
4. What are all my options, including doing nothing for now?
5. For each medicine: what is it for, how long, side effects, interactions?
6. What will it cost in total?
7. What warning signs mean I should come back or go to hospital?
8. When is my next visit and what will we check?
9. Is a second opinion reasonable?
10. May I have copies of my reports, scans and a discharge summary?

Details: [How to judge medical advice](../00-start-here/01-how-to-judge-medical-advice.md), [Second opinions](../08-decisions/second-opinions-and-treatment-decisions.md).

## Card 3: Reading any lab report in ten steps

1. Check **name, age, sex, date and lab**.
2. Note **conditions**: fasting? ill? supplements (biotin)? exercise? period?
3. For each abnormal result: the **test, unit and range printed on this report**.
4. **How far outside** the range?
5. Read it **with its partner tests** (TSH with free T4; ALT with AST; Hb with MCV and ferritin; creatinine with eGFR).
6. Think of **non-disease causes** (dehydration, illness, medicines, time of day).
7. **Compare with earlier reports.**
8. Does it fit **your symptoms**?
9. **Urgent, repeat, or just mention** at the next visit?
10. **Write your questions** and bring all reports.

A reference range covers about **95 of 100 healthy people**, so a mildly abnormal value is common and often needs only a repeat. Details: [How lab reports work](../03-tests/01-how-lab-reports-work.md), [Worked examples](../10-practical/worked-examples.md).

## Card 4: Example ranges (check your own report)

> [!WARNING]
> **Examples only.** Ranges vary by lab, method, age, sex and pregnancy. These are not diagnostic cut-offs or treatment targets.

| Test | Example range (adults) |
|---|---|
| **Haemoglobin** | Men about 13 to 17; women about 12 to 15 g/dL |
| **MCV** | about 80 to 100 fL |
| **Platelets** | about 150,000 to 400,000/µL |
| **TSH** | about 0.4 to 4.5 mIU/L |
| **Fasting glucose / HbA1c** | Normal: fasting below 100 mg/dL, HbA1c below 5.7%; prediabetes 100 to 125 and 5.7 to 6.4%; diabetes 126 mg/dL and 6.5% or more (ADA, confirm with repeat) |
| **ALT (SGPT)** | upper limit about 40 to 50 U/L |
| **Creatinine** | Men about 0.7 to 1.3; women about 0.6 to 1.1 mg/dL; look at **eGFR** |
| **LDL / HDL / triglycerides** | "Desirable" LDL under about 100, HDL over 40 (men) or 50 (women), triglycerides under 150 mg/dL; **your target depends on your overall risk** |
| **Blood pressure** | Normal under about 120/80; hypertension 140/90 or above (India) |
| **Sodium / potassium** | about 135 to 145 / 3.5 to 5.0 mmol/L |

Details: the test chapters in `03-tests` and the disease chapters in `02-diseases`.

## Card 5: Medicine safety checklist

- Know **what each medicine is for** and for **how long**.
- **Read the strip:** ingredient, strength, batch, expiry, licence, MRP. Buy from a **licensed pharmacy** and **keep the bill**.
- **Tell every doctor and pharmacist everything you take,** including herbs and supplements, and your **allergies, pregnancy, kidney and liver disease.**
- **Do not stop** blood pressure, diabetes, thyroid, heart, epilepsy, steroid or psychiatric medicines suddenly.
- **Antibiotics only when prescribed;** not for colds, cough or most diarrhoea.
- **Avoid regular NSAIDs** (ibuprofen, diclofenac) if you have kidney disease, ulcers or are on blood thinners; **check hidden paracetamol.**
- **Never share or save leftovers.**
- **Check for duplicates** (two tablets with the same ingredient).
- **Sick day:** ask which medicines to pause when you cannot eat or drink.
- **Report side effects.**

Details: [Medicines](../04-medicines/how-medicines-work.md) chapters, especially [Reading a prescription](../04-medicines/reading-a-prescription.md).

## Card 6: Red flags for being misled

- "**Cure** in days" for a long-term disease.
- "Stop your tablets."
- "**Doctors are hiding this.**"
- **Pressure to decide today** when it is not an emergency.
- A **package or discount** on a procedure.
- Only **testimonials**, no evidence.
- A seller who will not list the **ingredients**.
- **Forwards** telling you to share quickly.
- Antibiotic, steroid or "tonic" **kits** with no stated purpose.
- A **single scan or number** used to justify surgery or a stent for a stable condition.

**Do:** ask for the diagnosis, the evidence, alternatives and costs in writing; get a **second opinion**; check the claim in [Health misinformation](../09-indian-healthcare/health-misinformation.md).

## Card 7: Know your numbers

Write down (and update yearly) your: **weight and waist**, **blood pressure**, **fasting glucose or HbA1c**, **lipids**, **haemoglobin**, **creatinine and eGFR**, **TSH if relevant**, **ALT**, **vaccination dates**, **medicines**, **allergies**, **blood group**.

Keep **scans (images) and reports** in dated folders and bring them to visits. Details: [Worked examples and health records](../10-practical/worked-examples.md).

## Card 8: Habits that matter most

1. **No tobacco.**
2. **Move:** about 150 minutes of brisk activity a week plus strength work.
3. **Sleep** 7 to 9 hours.
4. **Eat** mostly home-cooked, half the plate vegetables and fruit; less sugar, salt and fried food.
5. **Limit alcohol.**
6. **Vaccinate** (children's schedule, yearly flu where advised, tetanus every 10 years).
7. **Know your screening** (blood pressure, sugar, cancer screening at the right ages).
8. **Wear a helmet and seat belt.**
9. **Look after your mind;** ask for help early.

Details: [Lifestyle](../07-prevention/lifestyle-activity-sleep-tobacco-alcohol.md), [Nutrition](../05-nutrition/nutrition-and-the-indian-diet.md), [Vaccines](../07-prevention/vaccines-and-immunisation.md), [Screening](../07-prevention/screening-and-checkups.md).

## Card 9: Quick facts on the big three (thyroid, sugar, liver)

**Thyroid:** TSH is the **brain's signal**; high TSH usually means an **underactive** thyroid. One mildly high TSH is often temporary: **repeat in about 2 to 3 months** with free T4. Thyroid tablets are not for weight loss. Biotin can distort results. → [Thyroid](../02-diseases/thyroid.md)

**Diabetes:** diagnosis needs a **confirmed** abnormal result. **HbA1c can mislead in anaemia and haemoglobin disorders.** Low sugar (below 70 mg/dL): **15 g fast sugar, recheck in 15 minutes.** Never stop medicines because a herb "cured" you. → [Diabetes](../02-diseases/diabetes.md)

**Liver:** ALT/AST show **injury**; albumin and INR show **working ability**. **"Fatty liver grade 1" is common;** the question is **scarring (FIB-4, FibroScan).** "Liver detox" and routine "liver protective" tablets are not proven. **Paracetamol limits and alcohol matter.** → [Liver and fatty liver](../02-diseases/liver.md)

## Card 10: Cholesterol, chest infections and cancer warning signs

**Cholesterol:** LDL is the main number, and **your target depends on your overall risk**. Statins are well studied; do not stop them without asking your doctor. A family history of early heart disease or very high LDL is a reason to ask about inherited cholesterol. → [Lipid profile](../03-tests/lipid-profile.md)

**Chest infections:** a cough with **fast breathing, chest pain, blue lips, or a child who cannot drink** needs a doctor the same day. Antibiotics do not treat colds or flu. → [Asthma, COPD and chest infections](../02-diseases/asthma-and-copd.md)

**Cancer:** a lump that does not go, a sore in the mouth for more than 3 weeks, unexplained weight loss, or bleeding that is not normal should be checked early. Screening that works is mouth, breast and cervix checks. Beware of "cures". → [Common cancers in India](../02-diseases/common-cancers-in-india.md)
